\title{Denoising Markov models for variable-length multimodal data:\\ an insertion process on an auxiliary state, and one clock per coordinate}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We consider data $\bx\in\X^{D}$ of variable length $D$, where the coordinate space $\X$ may itself be multimodal. The length and, more generally, the \emph{shape} of the data is carried by an auxiliary state $s$ in a discrete space $\Sa$; conditioned on $s$, the data is a tuple of $D(s)$ coordinates. Generation is a Markov process with two kinds of generators: an \emph{insertion process} on $\Sa$, driven by a global clock $t$, whose jumps create (or remove) coordinates; and, conditioned on the current $s$, one denoising generator per coordinate, each driven by its own clock $u_d$. A coordinate is created at clock zero, i.e.\ from the reference measure of its own process, so the only learned objects are the reverse rates on $\Sa$ and the reverse generators of the coordinates. The clocks may be synchronised into a single time variable, but nothing requires it: training on the product law of the clocks licenses every monotone clock policy at sampling time. Ordinary multimodal diffusion, masked diffusion, Edit Flows and OneFlow are instances.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setup}

\paragraph{Coordinate space.}
Let $\X=\X_1\times\dots\times\X_K$ be the state space of one coordinate; the factors are the \emph{modalities} (continuous, discrete, or otherwise). Each modality $k$ carries a \emph{noising process}, a Markov process on $\X_k$ with generator $\gen^{k}_{u}$ in its own time $u\in[0,1]$, whose fully noised law is a fixed reference $\pi_k$ reached \emph{exactly}. We write generation time throughout: $u=0$ is the reference end, $u=1$ the data end. Instances: the Gaussian path $x_u=u\,x_1+(1-u)\,\varepsilon$ with $\pi=\mathcal N(0,I)$, an absorbing (masked) CTMC with $\pi=\delta_{\mathrm{MASK}}$, any finite-time flow. A modality may be \emph{degenerate}: no noising process, its value is fixed when the coordinate is created (its clock jumps from $0$ to $1$). Write $\pi=\bigotimes_k\pi_k$.

\paragraph{Data space.}
\begin{equation}
\X^{*}\;=\;\bigsqcup_{D\ge 0}\X^{D}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Auxiliary state.}
Let $\Sa$ be a countable set. Every $s\in\Sa$ determines an index set of coordinates $\C(s)$ with $|\C(s)|=D(s)$: the length, and whatever else the shape of the data carries (an order, positions, a tree, a graph). Conditioned on $s$, the data is $\bx=(x_d)_{d\in\C(s)}\in\X^{D(s)}$. Examples: $\Sa=\N$ with $\C(n)=\{1,\dots,n\}$ and an exchangeable model (sets); $\Sa$ = strings with $\C(s)$ the positions (sequences); $\Sa$ = trees or graphs. A singleton $\Sa$ is a fixed shape and recovers ordinary multimodal diffusion.

\paragraph{Clocks.}
A global clock $t\in[0,1]$ drives $\Sa$; every coordinate $d\in\C(s)$ carries its own clock $u_d\in[0,1]$ (if wanted, one clock per modality of the coordinate, $u_{d,k}$). The \emph{augmented state} is
\begin{equation}
z=(s,\bx,\bu),\qquad \bx\in\X^{D(s)},\quad \bu\in[0,1]^{D(s)} .
\end{equation}

\section{Noising construction: independent given the data}
\label{sec:noising}

Fix a datum $(s_1,\bx_1)$. The noising construction consists of two independent ingredients.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Noising on $\Sa$.} A CTMC on $\Sa$ started at $s_1$, with rates $\mu_t(s\to s')$ (in noising direction: deletions of coordinates in the simplest case, in general any edit of the shape). Its law at global time $t$ is $K_t(s_1,\cdot)$. When a coordinate leaves $\C(s)$ its value is discarded.
\item \textbf{Noising of the coordinates.} Independently, every coordinate $d\in\C(s_1)$ runs its own noising process on its own clock: $x_d\mid x_{1,d}\sim k_{u_d}(x_{1,d},\cdot)$ with $k_u=\bigotimes_k k^{k}_{u}$ the transition kernel of $\gen^{k}$ (per modality, on the coordinate's clock).
\end{enumerate}
The joint law at times $(t,\bu)$ is therefore
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:product}
p_{t,\bu}(s,\bx\mid s_1,\bx_1)\;=\;K_t(s_1,s)\prod_{d\in\C(s)}k_{u_d}(x_{1,d},x_d),
\end{equation}
a product over coordinates \emph{whose number and identity are random and determined by $s$}. This is the only structural assumption.

\section{Generators}

\subsection{Insertion process on \texorpdfstring{$\Sa$}{S}}

In generation time the process on $\Sa$ runs with rates $\lambda_t(s\to s'\mid \bx,\bu)$, conditioned on the current data and clocks. A jump $s\to s'$ acts on the coordinates through the embedding
\begin{equation}
\iota_{s\to s'}(\bx,\bu;\xi)\;=\;\bigl((x_d)_{d\in\C(s)\cap\C(s')}\cup(\xi_d)_{d\in\C(s')\setminus\C(s)},\;(u_d)_{d\in\C(s)\cap\C(s')}\cup(0)_{d\in\C(s')\setminus\C(s)}\bigr):
\end{equation}
surviving coordinates keep their values and clocks, removed coordinates are dropped, created coordinates receive values $\xi_d$ and clock $0$. The generator is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:genS}
\rgen^{\Sa}_t f(s,\bx,\bu)\;=\;\sum_{s'\in\Sa}\lambda_t(s\to s'\mid\bx,\bu)\int\Bigl[f\bigl(s',\iota_{s\to s'}(\bx,\bu;\xi)\bigr)-f(s,\bx,\bu)\Bigr]\;\pi^{\otimes(\C(s')\setminus\C(s))}(\dd\xi).
\end{equation}
Created coordinates are drawn from the reference measure $\pi$ because they are created at clock $0$, where their law under~\eqref{eq:product} is $\pi$ independently of everything else.

\subsection{Coordinate generators conditioned on \texorpdfstring{$s$}{s}}

Conditioned on $s$, each coordinate $d\in\C(s)$ has a generator $\rgen^{(d)}_{u_d}[s,\bx,\bu]$ acting on $x_d$ only: the time reversal of $\gen$ at clock $u_d$, evaluated with the joint marginal~\eqref{eq:product}, i.e.\ conditioned on the whole state $(s,\bx,\bu)$. For a modality with a Gaussian path it is the velocity field $v(z;d)\approx\E[x_{1,d}-\varepsilon_d\mid z]$; for a diffusion, the reverse drift with the conditional score; for a CTMC, the reverse rate matrix with the conditional ratios. A clock policy assigns to every coordinate a non-decreasing $u_d(t)$ with $u_d=0$ at creation, $\rho_d=\dd u_d/\dd t\ge 0$; each coordinate generator also transports its own clock,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:gend}
\rho_d(t)\Bigl(\rgen^{(d)}_{u_d}[s,\bx,\bu]+\partial_{u_d}\Bigr).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Joint generator and reversal}

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:joint}
\rgen_t\;=\;\rgen^{\Sa}_t\;+\;\sum_{d\in\C(s)}\rho_d(t)\Bigl(\rgen^{(d)}_{u_d}[s,\bx,\bu]+\partial_{u_d}\Bigr).
\end{equation}
The two kinds of generators act on different variables ($s$ and the $x_d$), so the sum is the generator of a Markov process on the augmented state, and they interact only through their coefficients: $\lambda_t$ reads the current coordinates and clocks, $\rgen^{(d)}$ reads $s$ and the other coordinates.

\begin{theorem}[Reversal, informal]
\label{thm:reversal}
Under the noising construction of Section~\ref{sec:noising}, for any clock policy $\brho$ with creation at clock zero, the time reversal of the joint process is the Markov process with generator~\eqref{eq:joint}, where
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lambda}
\lambda_t(s\to s'\mid\bx,\bu)\;=\;\mu_t(s'\to s)\,\frac{p_{t,\bu}\bigl(s',(x_d)_{d\in\C(s)\cap\C(s')}\bigr)}{p_{t,\bu}\bigl(s,\bx\bigr)}
\end{equation}
is the CTMC reversal with the joint marginal, and each $\rgen^{(d)}$ is the single-modality reversal of $\gen$ at clock $u_d$ with the joint marginal.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The construction is a product of independent processes given the data, one on $\Sa$ and one per coordinate, each with its own time; the multi-time reversal decomposition of Diffuse Everything applied to the augmented state gives a sum of single-component reversals with the joint marginal. In~\eqref{eq:lambda} the created coordinates do not appear in the ratio because their marginal at clock $0$ is $\pi$, which cancels; the same fact makes the creation kernel in~\eqref{eq:genS} equal to $\pi$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What is learned]
Two families of objects: the reverse rates $\lambda_\theta(s\to s'\mid \bx,\bu)$ on $\Sa$, which in~\eqref{eq:lambda} is the known noising rate times a learned ratio (as in masked diffusion and Edit Flows), together with the types of created coordinates when the modalities are degenerate; and the reverse coordinate generators $\rgen^{(d)}_\theta$. No transition kernel between $s$ and the data is ever modelled: a policy that created a coordinate at $u_d>0$ would require, inside $\rgen^{\Sa}$, a learned conditional $p(x_d\mid s,\bx,\bu)$ at that noise level.
\end{remark}

\section{Synchronising the clocks}

Any clock policy turns~\eqref{eq:joint} into a time-inhomogeneous Markov process in the single variable $t$: the clocks are functions of $t$ and of the creation times $\tau_d$, and each coordinate generator is reparametrised by $\rho_d(t)$. Three policies are of interest.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Synchronised.} $u_d(t)=(t-\tau_d)/(1-\tau_d)$, so $\rho_d=1/(1-\tau_d)$ and every coordinate reaches $u_d=1$ at $t=1$: one horizon, and a coordinate created late is denoised faster. If no coordinate is created after $t=0$ (fixed shape) this is $u_d\equiv t$, the single-time multimodal model.
\item \textbf{Unit rate.} $u_d(t)=\min(t-\tau_d,1)$, $\rho_d=1$ while $u_d<1$; the run continues past $t=1$ until the last coordinate is clean.
\item \textbf{Phase-separated.} $\rho_d=0$ while the insertion process is active, then all coordinates denoise together: first the shape, then the content.
\end{itemize}
None of them is privileged by the theory. Which one is licensed is decided by the law of the clocks at training time, Section~\ref{sec:training}.

\section{Training}
\label{sec:training}

Sample a datum $(s_1,\bx_1)$, a global time $t\sim U(0,1)$ and clocks $u_d\sim U(0,1)$ independently for all $d\in\C(s_1)$; draw $(s,\bx)$ from~\eqref{eq:product}; minimise
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:loss}
\E\Bigl[\ell^{\Sa}\bigl(\lambda_\theta(\,\cdot\mid \bx,\bu),\ s,\ s_1\bigr)\Bigr]\;+\;\E\Bigl[\sum_{d\in\C(s)}\ell^{(d)}\bigl(\rgen^{(d)}_\theta[s,\bx,\bu],\ x_{1,d},\ u_d\bigr)\Bigr],
\end{equation}
where $\ell^{\Sa}$ is the loss of the reversal on $\Sa$ (a Bregman divergence for rates, cross-entropy for the types of created coordinates) and $\ell^{(d)}$ is the standard loss of the coordinate's process at its clock (flow matching, denoising score matching, or the masked-diffusion cross-entropy), each evaluated at the joint state.

\begin{remark}[Coupled versus decoupled clocks]
Training with the product law $t\otimes\bigotimes_d u_d$ places training states everywhere in the time cube and licenses every clock policy at sampling time. Training instead with the push-forward of one policy, $u_d=\varphi(t,\tau_d)$, places them on the surface swept by that policy and licenses that policy only. Same model class, different law of the training times.
\end{remark}

\section{Design axes}

An instance has to choose: (i) $\Sa$ and its noising CTMC $\mu$ (which shape is grown and by which edits); (ii) which modalities of $\X$ are degenerate and which carry a noising process; (iii) the clock policy, a sampling-time choice under product-law training; (iv) the granularity of the clocks (per coordinate, per modality of a coordinate, per scalar), which decides which conditioning tasks are native time paths (a given value is a value held at clock $1$); (v) shape as a process on $\Sa$ versus shape as a value (a fixed maximal length with an ``absent'' symbol in $\X$), which the framework contains as $\Sa$ a singleton and which moves the modelling of absence into the coordinate processes.

Permutation invariance is not part of the framework: it enters only through $\Sa=\N$ and an equivariant parametrisation of $\lambda_\theta$ and $\rgen_\theta$. For sequences, $\Sa$ is the set of strings with positioned edits and the coordinate machinery is unchanged; for graphs, nodes and edges are coordinates with their own clocks.

\begin{example}[Layouts]
$\X=\mathcal Y\times\R^4$ with the class $y$ degenerate and the box $x$ on the Gaussian path; $\Sa=\N$ with exchangeable coordinates. The noising CTMC deletes each coordinate at an i.i.d.\ time with $\Pr(\tau\le t)=\kappa(t)$; by~\eqref{eq:lambda} its reversal is an insertion process with intensity $\lambda_t(n\to n+1\mid\bx,\bu)=\frac{\kappa'(t)}{1-\kappa(t)}\,\E[\,D(s_1)-n\mid z\,]$, and the class of the created coordinate is drawn from a learned $p_\theta(y\mid z)$; the box of the created coordinate is $\mathcal N(0,I)$. Each box has the generator $\rho_d(t)\,v_\theta(z;d)\cdot\nabla_{x_d}$ on its own clock. The synchronised, unit-rate and phase-separated policies are the three of Section~4.
\end{example}
